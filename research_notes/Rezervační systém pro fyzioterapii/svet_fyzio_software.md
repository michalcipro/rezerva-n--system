# Best-in-class international physiotherapy / allied-health practice-management and online-booking software (state 2025–2026)

## 1. Which platforms lead for physio/allied health and why (market position, G2/Capterra ratings, pricing, regions, EU/GDPR data hosting)?

### Takeaway
Jane (Canada) and Cliniko (Australia) are the most consistently top-rated cloud platforms for physio-centric clinics. Jane has about 500+ Capterra reviews at 4.8/5; Cliniko scores 4.7/5 on a small sample and reports 65,000+ users. They are also the only international leaders researched here with confirmed European data residency: Jane hosts UK/Europe accounts in London, and Cliniko hosts EU accounts in Ireland and UK accounts in the UK.

The rest of the field splits into:
- **AU/NZ challengers:** Nookal, Zanda (ex-Power Diary), splose, Halaxy, Coreplus.
- **UK-focused systems:** Semble, TM3, Pabau, WriteUpp.
- **US rehab-therapy EMRs:** WebPT, Prompt Health.
- **A coaching-style platform:** Practice Better.
- **An exercise-prescription add-on:** Physitrack/PhysiApp.
- **EU patient marketplaces:** Doctolib in FR/DE, and Docplanner, which runs ZnámýLékař.cz in CZ. These are markedly more expensive per seat, and Doctolib charges setup fees.

## 2. Booking-engine specifics: slot intervals, durations, buffers, practitioner-specific services, rooms/resources, recurring/treatment plans, group classes, online booking rules, waitlists, smart slot offering, double-booking, multi-location

### Takeaway
Jane has the richest documented online-booking rule engine:
- Three slot-start modes, including "based on shortest treatment" gap-avoidance, plus a "cluster" mode that offers slots adjacent to existing bookings.
- A per-day cap on displayed openings.
- A minimum-notice window with a "contact to book" fallback, a rolling horizon per practitioner and a per-client upcoming-appointment limit.
- Per-practitioner or per-treatment buffers, room/resource constraints and classes.
- Fully automatic waitlist offers with an admin buffer and an exclusive hold.

Cliniko deliberately derives slots from just four inputs: availability, default appointment type, max appointments per day segment, and lead time. It offers group appointment types and a manual waitlist. Other vendors add useful patterns: splose's patient-joinable waitlist and multiple booking pages, Nookal's 5–30-minute per-location grid, WriteUpp's 1–12-month booking horizon, and Prompt's series booking tied to plan-of-care.

### Cited Findings

**Slot generation, intervals and "smart"/gap-minimising offering**
- Jane's "Online Booking Start Time" has three modes — [Jane guide: Lesson 6 Online Booking](https://jane.app/guide/lesson-6-online-booking); [Jane guide: Practitioner Step 4 Online Booking](https://jane.app/guide/practitioner-step-4-online-booking):
  - **Sequentially (default):** slots are offered back to back based on treatment length. Long treatments can leave start times "pretty far apart" even on an empty day.
  - **Fixed interval:** e.g., every 30 minutes, so a 90-minute appointment appears every 30 minutes.
  - **"Based on my shortest treatment" (recommended):** any leftover gap always fits the shortest treatment. Example: with 30-, 45- and 60-minute treatments, every gap can take the 30-minute one.
- Jane's "Cluster Online Booking" offers appointments just before or after existing bookings, so clients book around what is already scheduled — [Jane guide: individual staff preference](https://jane.app/guide/online-booking-individual-staff-preference)
- Jane can cap how many openings are displayed per day, because "people can be wary of booking on a fully open schedule" — [Jane guide: individual staff preference](https://jane.app/guide/online-booking-individual-staff-preference); [Jane guide: Practitioner Step 4](https://jane.app/guide/practitioner-step-4-online-booking)
- Cliniko's online slots result from four settings working together: the practitioner's availability, their default appointment type, their "max appointments per day segment" setting, and lead time. Cliniko "doesn't have a setting that lets you hand-pick individual time slots" — [Cliniko help: time slots in online bookings](https://help.cliniko.com/en/articles/16332369-how-to-get-the-time-slots-you-want-to-show-in-online-bookings)
- Nookal sets the calendar grid per location (Setup → Locations → "Calendar Time Slot"):
  - The default is 15 minutes, selectable from 5 to 30 minutes.
  - Nookal advises choosing the shortest slot that fits all booking lengths, so bookings start and end on slot boundaries — [Nookal support: Changing Calendar Time Slot](https://support.nookal.com/hc/en-us/articles/9211462201743-Changing-Calendar-Time-Slot)
- Generic scheduling conventions in non-physio tools: Dubsado defaults to a 15-minute slot increment and 1-hour minimum notice — [Dubsado help](https://help.dubsado.com/en/articles/15546572-scheduler-intervals-and-buffers)

**Minimum notice, booking horizon, per-client limits**
- Jane: — [Jane guide: online booking grace period](https://jane.app/guide/online-booking-grace-period); [Jane guide: choosing what is offered online](https://jane.app/guide/online-booking-choosing-what-is-offered-online-locations-staff-shifts-treatments-etc-and-individual-staff-preferences)
  - "Do Not Allow Booking Within" sets the minimum advance notice, from a few hours up to a week.
  - With "Allow Same Day Booking" on, the same setting creates a granular same-day buffer, e.g., same-day bookings with at least 2 hours' notice.
  - "Show Openings as Contact to Book" decides whether slots inside the notice window are hidden or shown with a "call/email the clinic" prompt.
- Jane sets the booking horizon as "an online rolling availability value for each staff member". The Upcoming Appointments Limit (Settings > Online Booking) caps how many future appointments one person can hold — [Jane guide category: online booking (staging mirror)](https://staging4.jane.app/guide/category/online-booking); [staging2 mirror](https://staging2.jane.app/guide/category/online-booking)
- Cliniko lead time — [Cliniko help: lead time](https://help.cliniko.com/en/articles/1150377-specify-lead-time-in-online-bookings):
  - The default is set under Settings → Online bookings.
  - Each appointment type can override it in its "Lead time for online bookings" field (default "Use online bookings settings").

### Inferences
- Suggested core engine for the Czech clinic, distilled from the leaders:
  - Per-service duration plus optional per-service or per-practitioner post-buffer.
  - A selectable slot-start policy: sequential, fixed 15/30-minute, or "fit shortest service" (Jane), with an optional "cluster" mode to avoid fragmentation.
  - A per-practitioner daily display cap.
  - Minimum notice with an optional same-day rule and a "contact us" fallback.
  - A rolling horizon per practitioner.
  - A per-client cap on upcoming bookings.
- Physio + massage + conditioning needs **resource-aware scheduling**: therapy rooms, a massage room, gym/training floor capacity and equipment. Jane's resource-pool model, with all required resources checked and hard conflicts, is the pattern to copy. Semble-style soft warnings are weaker.

- Automatic waitlist offers are a differentiator only Jane (top plan) documents in detail. The 2h/15min admin buffer, exclusive hold, auto-release and first-come claim logic is a ready-made spec.
- Series/plan-of-care booking (Prompt; Doctolib FR treatment plans) is the physio-specific feature generic booking tools lack. Prompt's ">40% online, >85% utilization" case study suggests it pays off. Note this is a vendor claim.

## 3. Packages, memberships, credits, gift cards; card-on-file and automatic late-cancellation/no-show fees; how cancellation policies are displayed and enforced

### Takeaway
The leaders combine five mechanisms:
- Prepaid packages/memberships redeemable at online booking.
- A payment guarantee at booking: card on file, deposit or full prepayment.
- A configurable fee for late cancellation or no-show (% of price or flat).
- A self-service cancellation cutoff that mirrors the policy window.
- Policy text shown on the booking site.

Jane documents all of these, with online package sales and auto-applied memberships on its top plan. Cliniko enforces the cancellation window through its self-cancel link and online payments. No source confirmed fully automatic no-show charging in Jane or Cliniko: fees appear to be applied by staff when marking the appointment. Gift cards were not covered by any retrieved source.

### Inferences
- Recommended enforcement stack for the Czech clinic:
  1. Show policy text on the booking page and in the confirmation, with explicit acknowledgment.
  2. Require a card on file or a deposit for new clients and for high-value services (e.g., 60–90-minute massage, first physio visit), per Jane/Pabau.
  3. Set the self-cancel cutoff equal to the policy window (Cliniko).
  4. Make late-cancel/no-show fees configurable as % or flat (Jane).
  5. Apply fees staff-triggered by default, with optional auto-charge (SchedulingKit-style).
  6. Add an optional "grace allowance" (Kontur: 3 late cancellations per 12 months) and a "strike" rule that blocks online booking after repeated no-shows (Mudita).
- Prepaid packages and credits naturally enforce attendance: a late cancellation can consume a credit instead of charging a card. Cliniko's rule that credit-paid or fully prepaid appointments cannot be self-cancelled online shows the interplay must be designed explicitly.
- For conditioning training, memberships (Jane: fixed or unlimited sessions, auto-applied, auto-billed) and caps per week (Practice Better) fit "2× per week" training plans. Massages fit gift cards and packs, but no benchmark was retrieved for gift cards.
- Jane's insurer rule maps to a CZ design point: if any services are reimbursed by Czech health insurers, no-show/late-cancel fees must be billed to the client, never to the insurer. This is an inference; the Czech regulation was not researched.

- Not found for Zanda, Nookal, WriteUpp or Halaxy: card-on-file and auto-fee behaviour.
- No source on gift cards / vouchers for any researched platform, or on credit-wallet models beyond Cliniko's "account credits".
- Not researched: Czech/EU consumer-law and PSD2/SCA constraints on merchant-initiated card charges for no-shows.

### Inferences
- Booking ↔ clinical touchpoints worth designing in, derived from the patterns above:
  1. Service type determines which intake/consent pack is sent at booking (first visit vs follow-up vs massage vs training).
  2. A plan-of-care object (number of visits, frequency) drives series booking and "unscheduled remaining visits" alerts (Prompt).
  3. A home-exercise-program link is attached to the visit, either via a Physitrack-type integration or an in-house library.
  4. Outcome measures are scheduled at intake/discharge.
  5. Telehealth is an optional visit modality.
- Exercise-program content (thousands of videos) is a commodity bought via integration (Physitrack) rather than built. A custom Czech platform could integrate with or link out to such a tool. Physitrack has a Spanish-language market presence; Czech localisation is unverified.

## 5. Client portal/app UX: what makes Jane's and Cliniko's online booking highly rated; reminders, confirmations, calendar sync

### Takeaway
The available evidence points to a few UX patterns behind the top ratings:
- Few steps and mobile-first flows (splose rebuilt its flow in Dec 2025 explicitly for fewer steps and better mobile).
- Automatic matching of returning clients without accounts (Cliniko).
- Self-service links in reminders (Cliniko cancellation link).
- A client portal with stored cards and Apple/Google Pay (Jane).
- Waitlist claiming by email link (Jane).
- "Social-proof" display of limited openings (Jane caps displayed openings because fully open schedules deter bookers).

Step-by-step flow details and calendar-sync behaviour for Jane and Cliniko could not be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Jane:

### Inferences
- UX requirements for the Czech booking front-end:
  - Mobile-first, three to four steps (service → practitioner/"anyone" → time → details/payment).
  - Guest booking with automatic record matching by e-mail/phone (Cliniko), with an optional account/portal for packages, saved cards and history (Jane).

  - Self-service cancel/reschedule links in every reminder, respecting the policy cutoff.
  - Waitlist join when no slot fits (splose), with automatic offers (Jane).
  - Honest availability display that does not imply false scarcity (Doctolib ruling).
  - Two-way SMS confirmation (Nookal gap) and email+SMS reminders (Cliniko cost benchmark 10¢/SMS).
  - Series booking for treatment plans and training blocks (Prompt).

## 7. Built-in e-mail marketing / newsletters or integrations

### Takeaway
Only US platforms had verifiable patient-engagement/marketing modules in the retrieved evidence: WebPT Reach (PRM) and Prompt Plus (automated follow-ups). Elsewhere, communication evidence is limited to transactional messaging: reminders, waitlist emails and cancellation links. ### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- For a private Czech clinic, the transactional layer matters most and is well benchmarked: confirmations, reminders, self-cancel links, waitlist offers, package-expiry and "time to rebook" nudges. Newsletters can be delegated to an external ESP (e.g., Mailchimp/Ecomail-type tools) as long as marketing consent is captured separately from service messages (GDPR).
- A reactivation workflow (WebPT Reach-style) is the highest-value "marketing" feature for physio: patients who dropped out of a treatment plan, or training clients whose package lapsed.

### Gaps
- Not verified: built-in newsletter/bulk e-mail or marketing integrations (Mailchimp, Zapier) for Jane, Cliniko, Nookal, Zanda, splose, WriteUpp, Semble, TM3 and Pabau; whether Pabau's marketing suite applies to physio; push notifications.
